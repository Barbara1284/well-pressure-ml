Predicting the continuation of oil-well pressure recovery curves.

A machine-learning case study that reconstructs temporally unobserved pressure segments from partial curves, comparing Random Forest, XGBoost, MLP and TabPFN-3.

The challenge

Pressure recovery tests may be only partially observed. The goal was to use the available temporal segment of each well curve, together with operational, petrophysical and temporal features, to estimate the subsequent unobserved pressure trajectory.

This is a temporal forecasting/reconstruction formulation, not generic random missing-value imputation.

Experimental design

Models

Random Forest · XGBoost · MLP · TabPFN-3

Optimization

Optuna/TPE · 50 trials for RF, XGB and MLP · final 20% of each training curve reserved for temporal validation.

Evaluation

Five independent runs with the same seeds across models. Metrics: RMSE, MAE, R², RMSE per curve, residuals and error by prediction horizon.

Interpretability

SHAP global importance, beeswarm analysis and 3D response surfaces.

What the prediction looks like

Representative curves were selected objectively at the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, 90th and 95th percentiles of per-curve RMSE.

Where the model becomes less certain

The reconstruction error generally increases with temporal distance from the last observed point. This finding is central to responsible use of the model in engineering workflows.

Explainable AI

Reservoir pressure is the dominant feature, followed by bottomhole pressure and temporal descriptors. The learned relationships are therefore connected to physically meaningful aspects of the well/reservoir state.

Practical value

Potential uses

Reconstruct incomplete tests, estimate subsequent pressure behavior, support test interpretation and flag deviations as new observations arrive.

Important limitation

The model is a support tool. Longer prediction horizons are less accurate, and field deployment requires uncertainty controls and validation on new wells/reservoir conditions.
